# 0067 Outside OAuth sign-ins, a grant per person

- **A server that takes only an OAuth sign-in** is an outside MCP server (0056) whose calls carry one person's grant: that person's sign-in to that party, naming their outside account and holding its access and refresh tokens, kept by the gateway alone (no session holds it, no token passes through, MCP's token handling).
- **Whose grant a call uses.** The asking ship's owner (0045) is its person. A ship owned by argo is answered at once with an error result saying no person can sign in for it. Every ship a person owns, their person ship included, calls under their grant, within reach and the tool switches; a change of owner moves the ship to the new owner's grant from its next call. The grant is checked when the call is handed over, never earlier.
- **Registration.** The gateway registers one client per party by dynamic registration (RFC 7591), bound to the issuer that issued it; every grant of the party is under that client. A server whose authorization server offers no dynamic registration is refused, for now, and nothing is kept. The client follows the MCP authorization spec: discovery from the protected resource, PKCE S256 or refused, `resource` in authorize and token requests, `state` checked, `iss` checked where stated. Outside scopes are the spec's default: the 401's `scope`, else all `scopes_supported`, with `offline_access` and `openid` when listed.
- **Signing in.** Every account of the fleet with a person ship signs in for themselves on the server's page on the gateway, whenever they choose; changing the party still needs its scopes. Adding a server ends with the approver signing in; their grant reads the tools for the proxy ship's statement and switches, later statements use any usable grant, the most recently used first. A new sign-in replaces the person's earlier grant. Where the server gives an `openid` subject, a sign-in as an outside account another person's grant already names is turned away with why.
- **No usable grant.** A call whose person holds none (never signed in, `invalid_grant`, revoked, ended unused) is answered at once with an error result naming the missing sign-in and the page, then handled; the server never sees it. The person is told once per party, by a text message from the gateway's ship to their person ship with the page's link, and again only after a sign-in in between. A 401 refreshes and calls again once; a 403 `insufficient_scope` answers an error result naming the page to sign in again, the grant kept.
- **Keeping a grant alive.** The gateway refreshes ahead of the access token's end and on a schedule while the grant is idle, one refresh at a time per grant, storing the new refresh token before it is used.
- **Ending a grant.** The person removes their own; an actor holding `gateway:approve` within manage reach of the proxy ship removes anyone's. A person's grants go when their person ship is retired or their account is disabled (pagasae tells the gateway, as it does for a Slack link). Retiring the party drops every grant and deletes its client where the server offers it. A grant is revoked at the server where it offers revocation, then dropped.
- **Who sees grants.** Each person sees their own: the outside account, when signed in, last used and its state. Every actor holding `gateway:approve` within manage reach of the proxy ship sees all of them, never a token. The proxy ship's verified facts keep the issuer only (0059).

Why: a grant per person is the only model where the outside service's own audit names the causing person, while core's history names the asking ship (Thomas: "This is the building block type of work that would allow us to sell actual value pieces"). A client per party keeps one fleet's grants apart from another's, and each person consenting to it is the per-client consent a proxy owes against the confused deputy. Answering at once loses no call and tells the asking ship the cause; a leaver's outside account stops acting for the fleet when their access does. The full flow and scenarios are in [the design](../design/oauth-servers/oauth-servers.md).

Rejected: one shared grant owned by one human; the fleet's own record as the only trace; one client per issuer per installation; pre-registered clients only, and for now a pre-registered client the approver enters (CIMD comes when a server offers it); keeping a call until its person signs in, or the five attempts; refreshing only when a call needs it; the approver picking outside scopes; argo signing in for the ships it owns; the person picking which of their ships use their grant; grants going only when the person ship is retired; the page only for accounts whose ships reach the proxy ship; two persons on one outside account.
