# 0045 Owners

- Every ship but argo and the viewer has one owner: an active person ship (0044), agent, app or argo. A person ship owns itself (0052).
- At commission the owner is the commissioner itself when it is a person ship; the account's person ship when an account commissions (0057); the person ship argo names when argo, signed in to the console, commissions an agent ship: an active person ship, never argo, refused without one, the one argo named last preselected; otherwise the commissioner's owner, argo by default, so a ship an agent ship commissions, such as an orchestrator's, gets the orchestrator's owner. Plugin and trierarch ships are argo's. An authorised commissioner with `fleet:on-behalf` may name the actual requester with `ownerOf` and select `ownership: requester` to make that active person ship, agent or app the owner; omitted or explicit `requester-owner` inherits its owner, or, commissioning an app ship (0044), the owner its approver picked, a person's ship (the gateway names the person inside who answers for the outside party, 0056). `OwnerSet` on the new ship names the owner and how it was set.
- `ownership` requires `ownerOf` and cannot accompany `owner`. Both ownership modes retain existing kind checks and take scope authority from the live requester or a recorded rights approval (0046), never the selected owner. `OwnerSet` records `askingShip` for requester mode and `askingShipOwner` for inherited mode, with `askingShipId` and `ownerShipId`.
- Omitted and explicit `requester-owner` retain the existing commission hash. Only requester mode adds `ownership` to it; changing mode under the same key refuses `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`. A completed retry returns the existing ship without a secret or ownership/event changes, even after a later owner change or approval withdrawal. Refusals consume no commission key.
- An actor holding `fleet:owner` changes an owner, within its manage reach (`fleet.setOwner`, `OwnerChanged`, 0053), never a person ship's: it owns itself.
- Retiring an agent or app transfers its active direct children to its immediate owner. Retiring a person transfers its active direct children to argo, excluding its self-reference. Retired children and further descendants keep their owners. Every transfer writes `OwnerChanged { fromShipId, toShipId, why: 'ownerRetired' }` with the retiring call's actor in the retirement transaction, including lease end and all cleanup; failure rolls it all back. Adjacent ancestor retirements inherit the current parent under the ownership lock. Stored questions keep their original destinations. Disabling an account does not transfer ownership.
- Ownership is acyclic: a person self-root is the only self-reference. `setOwner` and fresh commissions validate the proposed owner and its ancestors within the fleet; missing, retired or ineligible owners refuse `INVALID_OWNER`, and direct or multi-edge loops refuse `OWNERSHIP_CYCLE`, without state or event writes. A same-owner change writes nothing. Owner writers, including commission, implicit person commission, retirement, fleet creation and deletion, take the fleet ownership lock before meeting, schedule, ship and other subordinate locks. State and owner events commit together.
- Ownership reach is direct: an owner reaches its immediate children for every verb; ancestors gain no reach through the chain. A child sends to its direct owner through the visible `sendToOwner` built-in, without reverse read/manage reach; scopes and all other send checks apply (0034).
- argo counts as a human here, as the fallback: a human crews argo (0012). A ship that falls to argo is a failing made visible, never a normal path (0057).
- The owner is used in four places, all through what exists:
  - questions and gates go to the owner's ship (0038);
  - trouble a ship reports goes to the owner's ship (0026);
  - the owner's ship reaches the ships it owns for every verb (a built-in rule, 0034), and rules may select by owner;
  - a person's app's statement is trusted for a person only when the app belongs to that person (0047).
- Left out until a need shows: lists of argo-owned ships to work through, handover or acceptance steps, reminders or reviews, owner-only request rules, a person record beside the person's ship.

Why: "a human is always responsible. Gates are clear, but every ship needs to be maintained. It's like application owners in current IT management." And: "we need to be VERY careful here that we don't introduce process due to process", so one field and its events, grown where needed. A person commissions from the console signed in as argo, which is no account, so the commission asks who answers for the ship at the moment it matters; otherwise its questions and permission asks reach argo, the fail-safe (0057), and no person.

Rejected: an owner label (a second source of truth beside the fact); an optional owner; for argo's console commissions, a person ship named once per console sign-in, or argo as owner when the fleet has no person ship; owners handing ships over between themselves; ownership as a person record apart from ships (a second identity).
