# 0040 Delivery protocol

The fleet's promise ends when the agent has confirmed it received the message. A ship with a computer side (the trierarch, or the Aeolus Fleet plugin's watcher and hooks) is pushed; any other ship pulls.

- **Push receive** (`pushReceive`): the computer side takes up to `max` deliveries. A pending one becomes `received`, in flight with the crew's ship and lease, with no attempt counted and `DeliveryReceived`. Its own received or handed ones come back first, changing nothing, with `DeliveryReceived` marked `again`, so a restart or a lost answer loses nothing.
- **Pushed lease**: a lease is pushed from its first push receive, marked once with `LeasePushed`, until it ends; a new lease pulls until its own first push receive. On a pushed lease the agent's plain `receive` answers nothing and claims nothing, pending deliveries included.
- **Who wakes it** (interop 5.1): the first push receive may state its waker, one of `trierarch`, `pluginWatcher`, `personApp`, `gateway`, `adapter`, kept with the lease and `LeasePushed`; a later one changes nothing. The ship's page names it; a lease that states none reads as its trierarch or else the plugin watcher, and a lease that never push received reads "Pull: nobody wakes it".
- **Hand-over** (`handed`): the computer side reports each hand-over to the agent with its hand-over number. A number higher than the last counts one attempt and writes `DeliveryHanded`; the same or a lower one is a lost answer and changes nothing, also once acknowledged. The fifth attempt is answered undeliverable and nothing is handed. It may name its channel (1 to 32 of a-z, 0-9, `/`, `.`, `-`, such as `tmux`, `email`, `tools/call`), kept in `DeliveryHanded` and shown in the delivery's history.
- **Attempts**: one per hand-over on a pushed ship, one per plain receive on a pull ship; a push receive counts none.
- **Confirmation**: the hook's `ack` on a received or handed delivery is the agent's confirmation (0001); a received one is taken too, so a lost answer to `handed` never blocks it.
- **Lease end** returns received and handed deliveries to pending, their claim and hand-over number cleared and their attempts kept; a retire abandons those to the ship. An acknowledged one ends acknowledged, handled unknown: its crew's later reports are refused as lease ended.
- **Turn** (`turn`): the hooks report the session's turn, idle or busy, with the time they saw it. The lease keeps it at that time with `TurnReported`; the same turn again, or a report older than the turn held, changes nothing, and a time later than now is taken as now.
- **Handled** (`handled`): the turn that took an acknowledged delivery ended by itself; `DeliveryHandled`. A delivery not acknowledged yet is refused, so a ping not answered with pong stays handed.
- **Once more** (`handed` with `isOnceMore`): a confirmed message whose turn or session died is handed once more, the one retry. It stays acknowledged, counts no attempt, and writes `DeliveryHanded` marked `onceMore`; its repeat changes nothing and a second once more is refused.
- **Not handled** (`notHandled`): the turn it was handed once more to died too; `DeliveryNotHandled`. It shows in Needs attention with the undeliverable ones, where Resend and Dismiss take it as they take an undeliverable one. Only a delivery handed once more is reported not handled, or one handed and not confirmed whose crew judged it unsafe to hand again and says why (the reason is then required): an outside call that may have run and lost its answer (interop 5.2), where handing again until the fifth attempt would be a blind retry.
- **Clocks** (#586): the fleet runs them for pushed leases from the states and the turn reported, so a computer that died leaves the source of truth true. A delivery is overdue when handed and not confirmed for 10 minutes, or pending or received for 10 minutes while the turn is idle and 60 while it is busy, counted from the latest of its state, the turn and the push (no turn reported is idle since the push). argo and the ship's owner are told once per lease and oldest overdue delivery, with `DeliveryOverdue`, which names the owner at that time; the watch plugin raises it as an alert to the owner's ship (monitoring design), so the owner hears of it as well as argo; on Health it is stalled in flight (0042) until it clears by itself once the delivery leaves that state or the lease ends; no inbox message, since the fleet does not speak for a ship. Core passes every 30 seconds; what argo was told is stored, so a restart or a second pass tells nothing again. A person ship's lease runs no clocks: a person away is not stuck (0044).
- **Re-read** (`delivery`, `messages:receive`, #1048): the crew reads an acknowledged delivery its lease holds, not yet handled, again, whole, as `receive` hands one, with its state; once more included, on every lease kind. It hands, counts and records nothing. One of its lease in another state is refused with that state (`DELIVERY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`); another ship's, another lease's or an unknown one is `DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`.
- Handled and not handled hold the crew's lease and are reported by the crew holding the delivery; reporting either again changes nothing.

- **Force** (`messages:force`, 0046): a forced delivery comes first, before older ones, in receive and push receive, and the delivery records that it was forced. The trierarch stops a busy turn and hands it over as a new prompt; a session crewed by hand gets it at its next tool call or Stop, and a hand-crewed Codex task, whose hooks put nothing into a running turn, at its next idle. Force is not a promise, it is a best effort: the computer side hands a forced message over as soon as it can, never later than a plain one. The delivery a stopped turn was working on is handled when the forced turn ends, since the forced message may say to stop that exact work.

Why: the computer side is a third party between the fleet and the agent. Its steps get states of their own instead of being hacked into the pull protocol, and the fleet, the one source of truth, holds every step.

Force means act now, as soon as the computer side can. Rejected: a forced message waiting for the turn to end where the computer side can stop the turn (it is not force); a stopped turn's delivery handed once more (stopping is not dying); the trierarch running the clocks (a session crewed by hand, or a trierarch that is down, would go untold); an inbox message to argo for an overdue delivery; the computer side acking for the agent (the promise would end before the agent has the message); counting an attempt per push receive (a restart would spend attempts); a plain receive on a pushed lease taking deliveries (the agent would see a message twice); the gateway keeping a copy of an opened payload instead of a re-read (it would flood the gateway's small database); telling once more from a higher hand-over number alone (a lost answer to `handed` followed by the hook's ack would make a repeat look like once more); a not handled message marked handled by default (handled only ever means a turn that took it ended by itself).
