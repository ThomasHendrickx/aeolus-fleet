# 0042 Stuck deliveries

A delivery is stuck when it waits on something only a ship, a crew or the operator can change (#304). The fleet reads its stuck deliveries, how long they wait and how that went over 7 days, as facts with no thresholds (0016), for the console's Health and the installation's fleets.

- **Causes,** one per delivery, the first that holds:
  1. **Undeliverable:** not dismissed. Its place is the ship it was last handed to, else its type.
  2. **No ship can take:** pending to a type with no active ship that may claim it (0041, 0034). Its place is the type.
  3. **Waiting for crew:** pending to a ship awaiting crew (never a person ship, 0044, nor a trierarch ship whose computer is not yet set up, 0026), or to a type whose every active ship that may claim it awaits crew. Its place is the ship, or the type.
  4. **Stalled in flight:** overdue, its pushed clock ran out (0040), whether or not argo was told of it yet; or handed on a plain lease whose session was last seen no later than the hand-over, 10 minutes or more ago (the pushed lease's not confirmed clock). Its place is the ship. Never at a person ship: a person away is not stuck (0044), so handed and not opened stays a fact with its age.
- **Since:** how long it waits counts from its state: undeliverable from when it became so, pending from its latest return else its send, in flight as the overdue clocks count it.
- **Reads:** stuck now, by cause and place, each with its count and oldest since; stuck per hour over the last 7 days; deliveries acknowledged in the last 24 hours, with the median from send to acknowledgement; and, for each place that is a ship, the computer whose trierarch its crew request is assigned to.
- **The stuck snapshot** (performance design, section 5): the 30 second pass closes each hour once, per fleet, under an advisory lock: from the latest hour it folds the events after it, in their fleet's order, up to the first past the next hour mark, and counts by the same rule at that mark, by cause, place and message parties (so a read keeps only what its caller reads, 0034), with the waits acknowledged in the hour as counts per log-spaced duration bucket. It keeps 168 hours and the open deliveries and turns as of the latest. A closed hour stands as it was counted: a plain lease's last seen is one column, read at the close, and a change of the rule counts from its next close on. An event that commits after its hour was closed lands in the next hour.
- **Reads** fold the events after the latest hour and apply the rule at now; stuck per hour is the closed hours and now, acknowledged the closed hours of the last 24 and the open one, the median from their buckets. A fleet without a snapshot, or whose latest hour is 168 hours old, starts its snapshot at the oldest of the 168 hours from the deliveries open then and closes forward hour by hour, so no close reads more than an hour of events; until its first hour is written, the replay of its events answers its reads. A new fleet starts with an empty hour at its creation.
- **Not handled** is execution, not delivery (0001): it is no cause, and the health reads leave it out.

Why: health must show what is stalled, where, since when and whether it gets better or worse, and the operator needs ages and a trend for that. The rule reuses the 10 minute clock rather than a threshold of its own. A snapshot, since replaying 7 days on every read grows with the fleet's history (#1117); a closed hour is true for everything before it, so no read or close looks at an event it already holds.

Rejected: the ages kept out of the console; a plain stall by its own clock or a heartbeat (0010); counting only the overdue deliveries argo was told of (one per lease); events kept by the server for stuck; a projection kept current by a consumer with a checkpoint, a materialized view replaying 7 days on each refresh, and counters per hour moved by events, which cannot see stuck by time passing (performance design, section 4).
