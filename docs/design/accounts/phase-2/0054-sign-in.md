# 0054 Sign-in through pagasae (draft)

- pagasae signs every account in. Until the organisation connects a directory, by a sign-in link mailed to the account's email. Once it connects one (Google Workspace, Entra; a later slice), every account signs in with its identity, and only the accounts allowed to sign in as argo keep the sign-in link beside it, as the way in when the identity provider fails. An email with accounts in several organisations is asked which one.
- pagasae is the one identity provider of the gateway and of plugin pages (OpenID Connect). The gateway's identity provider per fleet goes; interop I-4's "the gateway's OAuth server federates to the company IdP" becomes "through pagasae".
- **Gateway sign-in.** The gateway has one address for every fleet. Its authorize request sends the human to pagasae, which answers the account, its organisation, and each fleet where the account has a person ship. One fleet: straight in. Several: the human picks one. None: refused, "ask an organisation admin". The token the gateway issues names the fleet it was issued for (RFC 8707); the gateway crews the person ship and serves every client of the same human through that one lease. At every gateway sign-in pagasae puts the person ship's scopes and labels right against its groups (0052).
- **The gateway's own page.** A human who signs in with no AI or outside app reaches their person ship on the gateway's own page: their messages and questions, answering, sending, and asking for an outside MCP server (0056). Basic but functional, and far from ugly.
- **A plugin page** (squadrons, the networking plugin, the gateway's approving) signs a person in through pagasae too, gets their person ship in that fleet, and checks its scopes (0053). argo and the viewer keep `console.session`.
- **Console sign-in as argo.** An organisation admin allows accounts to sign in as argo of each fleet. An allowed account signs in at pagasae and gets a sign-in ticket (0020) that names it; the console sign-in keeps the account, the console shows it, and every event argo's calls write carries it in its details, so the events say which human acted as argo. Still one console sign-in as argo at a time: a new one ends the last, whoever's (0012).
- **argo's own sign-in.** Core keeps an email and password that sign in as argo, with no organisation: `fleet:init` asks for them, a server command resets them and ends argo's console sign-in. It is a sign-in of the ship argo, not an account and not an operator account (0050): the one-line self-hosted setup, and a way in that depends on nothing. A self-hoster adds pagasae when they want an organisation (argo connects it, 0051) and keeps argo's own sign-in beside it. A hosted fleet has none: its console serves no password form (0020). The console sign-in says which way it came in.
- A ship is like an email inbox: which client reads it does not matter to the network. Its exceptions, each on purpose: a ship has one crew at a time (0010), so a human's clients share the gateway's one lease; argo opens only in core's console (0012); the viewer only with a viewer ticket (0022); a pushed lease confirms deliveries its own way (0040); notices and the guide stay argo's console's.

Why: Thomas: the gateway signs people in through the organisation's accounts "until an outside directory is connected", with one address "as Microsoft 365 does". Several humans may need argo, and the events must say which (G9). The way in without an identity provider is the one Microsoft and Google both keep (`accounts.md` part 2.2). argo's own sign-in keeps the self-hosted setup to one command and one human, and keeps argo reachable when everything else fails, which is argo's reason (0053).

Rejected: the gateway federating to each identity provider itself; an endpoint per fleet behind the one address; the human naming the fleet; one account per fleet allowed as argo; every account keeping the sign-in link once a directory is connected (a leaver the directory suspended still signs in); identity only, for every account (no way in when it fails); pagasae required for every self-hosted fleet; a person page in core (0043).

Changes: 0012 (sign-in as argo by account or argo's own sign-in, the console sign-in names its account), 0020 (the sign-in ticket names its account), 0043 (plugin pages sign people in through pagasae), interop I-4.
