Story

One fleet. Every message delivered.

Aeolus is the network your people, AI agents and business apps work on together. It carries every message to the one it was meant for, and asks a person when the decision is theirs. Professional first. The character is in how calmly it does that.

Character in motion
  1. A message lands: a light travels, the name it was meant for lights up, then settles.
  2. Busy: more lights, never faster ones. Calm under load.
  3. A question waits for a person: an amber ring stays until they answer. No badge counting up.
  4. Something is wrong: coral, once, saying what happened and who acts. Never a red wall.
1. The old story

Someone opened the bag.

Aeolus, keeper of the winds, gave Odysseus every wind tied up in one bag. The crew opened it, sure there was gold inside. The winds went everywhere at once, and the ship took ten years to get home.

2. Today

Every message has an address.

In most companies the winds still sit in one bag: inboxes, chat threads and tickets, opened by whoever happens to look. Aeolus carries each message to the person, agent or app it was meant for, and keeps it there until it is handled.

3. Tomorrow

One fleet, everyone aboard.

People, AI agents and apps work as one fleet. Agents do the work, apps keep the records, people make the calls. Everyone gets the message they need, and the crew is home for dinner.

Mark

An A that is a ship. The left leg is the mast, the right side is the sail, it stands on a hull, and the wind is the cut through the sail. One colour, flat, the same at every size.

How it is built
MastThe left leg of the A.
SailThe right side, its edge curved by the wind.
WindA cut, never a drawn line. It crosses the sail where the A has its bar.
HullWhat makes the letter a ship. Its keel rides two low swells.
Wordmark: the mark is the A
Lockups and sizes
48, 32, 24, 16 px. Never below 16.
  1. Clear space on every side is the height of the hull times two. Nothing enters it.
  2. One colour only: Slate, white, light slate on navy, or black. Never a gradient, a sky or a scene behind it.
  3. The wordmark is the mark as the A, followed by "eolus" in Jost 400 with -0.5 tracking. The x-height sits under the wind cut. Never retype it: use the file.
  4. Never rotate it, outline it, add the wind as a drawn line, or put a ship state on it. States belong to the screens.

Motion

Every movement means one thing, always the same thing. The mark only bobs: what a ship is doing shows in its name tag and in the lights between ships. Here they all are, live.

The vocabulary
Feel the difference: tap each one

The first feels like it answered you. The other two feel broken. Every tap in Aeolus answers inside the first.

Timing, at real speed
Event0.6 to 1.4 sA message travelling, a name lighting up. Once, then rests.
Breath3 to 6 s loopThe bob, an amber ring waiting.
Story4 to 5 s, onceA flow like the story demo. Plays when seen, tap to replay.
Drift7 to 18 s loopGlints on the water. Felt more than seen.
Urgentone 0.6 s pulse, then holdsOnly for alerts. Coral never keeps blinking.
Easing: the same move, four feels
Settle
A name lighting up, a ring settling.cubic-bezier(.3, 1.6, .5, 1)
Glide
A message travelling between ships.cubic-bezier(.35, 0, .25, 1)
Sway
The bob, an amber ring breathing.ease-in-out, back and forth
Steady
Glints drifting, a line that keeps time.linear
  1. The mark itself only bobs. States live on the name tag, never on the sail.
  2. One slow thing moves per ship. Everything else holds still.
  3. Events happen once and rest. Only states loop.
  4. The rest frame tells the story: a still or a screenshot shows the end, never the middle.
  5. Motion never blocks a click, never delays an answer.
  6. With reduced motion on, everything stands still at its rest frame and still reads.

Illustration

One kit, five parts, nothing else drawn. Illustrations explain the fleet: on empty states, first use, the website, pitch decks, docs and error pages. Inside working screens there are none: tags and lights do the job there.

The parts
Four kinds of ship

Every ship carries its kind on its name tag, everywhere: in the app's lists and filters, in illustrations and in the header. The symbol says what it is, the name says which one.

Four compositions
  1. Only the five parts. No people, faces, sky, sun, birds, buildings or props.
  2. Every name is one a business user recognises: a first name and team, an agent by its job, an app by its category, a plugin by what it connects.
  3. Colour only where it means something: blue for live, amber for waiting on a person, coral for a hand needed, grey for not landed.
  4. One idea per picture. If it needs a legend, it is a diagram: number the steps.
  5. Never inside tables, forms, settings or the inbox.
  6. Illustrations move with the Motion vocabulary: ships bob, glints drift, one flow plays at a time and then rests. A still shows the rest frame.

  1. Clear first, then a wink. If the wink costs clarity, drop it.
  2. No humour in alerts, lost data or security. There, plain and calm, with the next step.
  3. Never at the user's expense. The fleet may laugh at itself.
  4. Product words stay plain: fleet, ship, message, question.
  5. Names as people know them: a first name, an agent by its job, an app by its category, a plugin by what it connects. Never an id.
  6. One headline, one line. Names and numbers exact.
  7. Mythology as a wink, never a lecture: one reference, never explained.
